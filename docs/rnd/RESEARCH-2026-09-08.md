## 1. GPT Image 2 edits with reference images

### What OpenAI documents

**[VENDOR]** `gpt-image-2` supports the `v1/images/edits` endpoint.
Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-image-2

**[VENDOR]** `input_fidelity` is not a knob on this model.
The image generation guide states that for `gpt-image-2` you should "omit this parameter; the API doesn't allow changing it because the model processes every image input at high fidelity automatically."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation

Practical consequence for us: there is no fidelity dial to turn up when the silhouette drifts.
The only levers are the prompt, the reference set, and the retry loop.

**[VENDOR]** Multiple reference images are a supported first-class pattern.
The guide's own worked example passes "4 input images" to "generate a new image of a gift basket containing the items in the reference pictures."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation

**[VENDOR]** Masks are advisory, not authoritative.
"Masking with GPT Image is entirely prompt-based. The model uses the mask as guidance, but may not follow its exact shape with complete precision."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation

This is the single most important documented sentence for our silhouette problem.
OpenAI is explicitly telling us that a supplied shape is guidance and the model may not trace it.
Any design that assumes the canonical pendant PNG will be traced exactly is designed against the vendor's own stated behaviour.

**[VENDOR]** OpenAI documents the failure modes we are hitting.
"Although significantly improved, the model can still struggle with precise text placement and clarity."
"Despite improved instruction following, the model may have difficulty placing elements precisely in structured or layout-sensitive compositions."
"The model may occasionally struggle to maintain visual consistency for recurring characters or brand elements across multiple generations."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation

**[VENDOR]** Reference order matters and the first image is privileged.
The OpenAI cookbook on high input fidelity states that "while all input images are preserved with high fidelity, only the first one you provide is preserved with extra richness in texture."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity

Caveat I must flag: that cookbook is written for the earlier `input_fidelity="high"` path on `gpt-image-1`.
I did not find an OpenAI statement re-confirming the first-image privilege for `gpt-image-2`.
Treat "put the identity silhouette first" as a well-motivated hypothesis to A/B tonight, not as a documented `gpt-image-2` guarantee.

**[VENDOR]** The cookbook also recommends compositing rather than multiplying references.
For multiple faces it advises to "try combining all needed faces into a single composite image before sending the request."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity

This generalises to our case: if we need both the silhouette and a shot anchor, a single composited reference sheet may beat two separate references.
That is testable in one A/B tonight.

**[VENDOR]** The cookbook confirms what high fidelity is good at: "logos and other unique design elements remain true to the original assets" and "fabric textures, patterns, and logos remain consistent."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity

A name pendant is closer to a logo than to a scene, which is the most favourable framing available to us.

### What third parties report

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Numbered role labels are the documented working pattern on fal's prompting guide.
"Label each input image by role and reference the labels in the instruction."
Their worked pattern is literally "Image 1: base scene to preserve. Image 2: jacket reference. Image 3: boots reference."
Their stated rationale: "Labeling each input by role keeps compositing prompts grounded instead of making the model guess which image is content and which image is reference."
Source: https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Prompt ordering: fal recommends a fixed structure of scene, subject, specific details, intended artifact, constraints, "in that order."
Source: https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

Note the tension with our instinct.
fal puts constraints last, not first.
Our identity constraint is the whole product, so tonight is the right place to test identity-first versus fal's constraints-last ordering rather than assuming.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Explicit preservation lists work better than negatives alone.
fal's virtual try-on example uses a positive enumeration: "Preserve the face, body shape, pose, background, lighting, and framing from Image 1."
They pair it with the closing constraints "Keep everything else the same" and "No extra objects."
Source: https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

The reproducible pattern reported across guides is "split edits into change + preserve", use "change only X" and "keep everything else the same", and "repeat the preserve list each iteration to reduce drift."
Source: https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Reference count ceiling: fal states "The GPT Image family accepts up to 16 reference images for edits."
Source: https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

I found **no** credible source, vendor or third party, that quantifies at what reference count fidelity begins to degrade.
Stating a number here would be invention.
What I can say is that the only vendor-adjacent advice on the topic points the other way: composite into fewer images rather than adding more.
Source: https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The "over-modifies regions it was not asked to touch" failure is measured, not anecdotal.
GIE-Bench evaluates text-guided editing on two axes, "functional correctness" and "image content preservation, which ensures that non-targeted regions of the image remain visually consistent using an object-aware masking technique and preservation scoring."
Its headline finding on the OpenAI family: "GPT-Image-1 leads in instruction-following accuracy, but often over-modifies irrelevant image regions."
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11493

This is the academic statement of our exact defect: the model does the thing you asked and quietly redraws what you did not ask about.
GIE-Bench measured `gpt-image-1`, not `gpt-image-2`, so treat the direction as established and the magnitude on `gpt-image-2` as unmeasured.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** An empirical GPT-4o image study reports the sibling failure: models "underperform in scenarios where strict preservation of the original image's lighting, shading, and color tones is required."
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05979

### What I could not verify

- Whether "use image 1 as the exact outline" style instructions hold on `gpt-image-2` specifically. No reproducible published A/B found. This is exactly what tonight's lab should measure.
- Whether negative instructions ("do not add letters", "no extra charms") are more or less effective than positive preservation lists on this model. Guides recommend positive preservation lists; none of them ran a controlled comparison.
- Any published number for reference-count fidelity degradation.
- Whether `gpt-image-2` retains the first-image texture privilege from the `gpt-image-1` cookbook.

## 2. Jewelry product photography prompting - real camera, not CGI

### The structural finding

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Realism is not produced by adding detail; it is produced by internal physical consistency.
"Realism does not come from more detail. It comes from physical consistency: light, shadow, reflections, and materials that all belong to the same scene."
Source: https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

That reframes our prompt work.
Adding more adjectives is not the fix; making the light direction, the shadow direction, and the reflection direction agree in one sentence is the fix.

### Phrasing that reads as photography

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Lighting, stated with a direction and a consequence:
"Soft daylight entering from the left, casting a natural shadow to the right."
"One large studio softbox above and slightly in front of the product."
The guide stresses naming the consequence too, for example "reflections on the glass matching the same light direction."
Source: https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Lens and camera:
"Photographed at product level with a medium telephoto lens, natural perspective, and a shallow depth of field."
The stated rationale is that medium telephoto avoids the miniaturisation look that wide-angle and extreme macro produce.
Source: https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

Note the direct conflict with jewelry-specific guides, which push hard toward macro.
Blendnow's jewelry prompts use "tight macro crop", "close crop", "shallow depth of field", "flat-laid", "overhead shot", "three-quarter angle", "side profile."
Source: https://www.blendnow.com/blog/ai-jewelry-product-photography-prompts

Resolution for us: macro is right for a pendant close-up, but the guidance says to pair it with a named perspective and a contact shadow so it does not miniaturise.
Both sources are third-party; neither ran a controlled test.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The single highest-value phrase found, for grounding:
"A subtle contact shadow directly beneath the product, becoming softer and lighter further away."
The stated effect is that this one addition "signals physical grounding rather than floating."
Source: https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

Floating-object syndrome is one of our recurring defects and this is a cheap, specific fix to test.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Material phrasing: replace evaluative words with physical properties.
Instead of "luxury surface", write "brushed aluminum with fine directional grain and soft, diffused highlights."
Instead of generic glass, write "frosted glass that scatters light softly, with a faint highlight along the edge."
Source: https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

The transferable rule is that "luxury", "premium", "stunning", and "elegant" are evaluative and carry no optical information.
Grain direction, roughness, and highlight behaviour do.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Backdrop and surface, jewelry-specific vocabulary that is reported to work:
white marble, velvet, slate, linen, glossy black surfaces, matte ceramic, crushed ice, raw geode crystal, dark sand, seashells, handmade paper.
Verbatim example prompts from that source:
"Engagement ring on white marble with soft window light and shallow depth of field."
"Diamond solitaire on glossy black surface with dramatic side lighting."
"Stackable gold rings on linen cloth with dried eucalyptus sprigs."
Their prompt formula is "Product + Surface + Lighting + Mood + One Detail."
Source: https://www.blendnow.com/blog/ai-jewelry-product-photography-prompts

The "one detail" constraint is worth respecting - these prompts are short, and the source explicitly caps decoration at one element.

### Phrasing that triggers the CGI look

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The named red flags, all from the same reproducible guide:

- flawless, mirror-perfect reflections;
- impossibly clean surfaces;
- products floating without shadows;
- mathematically perfect, symmetrical composition, described as "dead-center symmetry and even spacing" reading artificial.

Their counter-phrasings are "positioned off-center in the frame", "subtle natural surface imperfections", and "natural variation in the reflections."
Source: https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

**[THIRD-PARTY]** A corroborating formulation from the render-prompt literature: "the fastest way to kill the plastic look is to name real materials with real texture words: honed, brushed, hand-scraped, nubby, veined", and for product images specifically "realistic metal reflections, subtle surface scratches, natural material grain."
Source: https://www.meltflexai.com/blog/ai-render-prompts

Important caution for a jewelry brand: "subtle surface scratches" is a realism cue for aluminium and concrete but is a **defect** on a new gold pendant.
Our equivalent should be polishing lines, a slightly uneven specular highlight, and micro-variation in the reflected environment, not damage.
That distinction is my inference from the sources, not something any source states about jewelry.

### What I could not verify

- No source I found ran a controlled A/B on `gpt-image-2` specifically for metal realism. Every jewelry prompt source above is a marketing blog for a competing tool and is **[THIRD-PARTY]** at best; none published failure rates.
- The claim that camera-body names ("shot on Hasselblad") improve realism appears constantly in prompt blogs and I found no controlled evidence for it. Treat as **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## 3. Arabic text fidelity in image generation

### Vendor position

**[VENDOR]** OpenAI's own developer guide is the conservative statement and it does not carve out an exception for any script: "Although significantly improved, the model can still struggle with precise text placement and clarity."
Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation

I found **no** OpenAI developer-docs statement giving a per-script accuracy figure.

### The 99% claim

**[UNVERIFIED]** A widely repeated claim states roughly 99% text rendering accuracy across Latin, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, Bengali, Arabic and other scripts, up from 90-95% in GPT Image 1.
It appears on many aggregator and vendor-adjacent blogs, for example https://picsart.com/ai-models/gpt-2/ and https://bibigpt.co/en/blog/posts/gpt-image-2-bibigpt-ai-image-generator .
I attempted to verify it against the OpenAI announcement at https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-images-2-0/ and received HTTP 403, so I could not confirm the primary source within the time budget.

Do not plan against 99%.
It is a repeated marketing number with no primary source I could reach and no published methodology, no per-script breakdown, and no definition of what counts as a character error.
Our own Arabic name QA rate is better evidence than this figure.

**[UNVERIFIED]** A comparative blog scores Ideogram 2.0 at 9/10 for Arabic with "correct letter connections, properly placed diacritics", and DALL-E 3 as "readable with minor imperfections."
Source: https://truescho.com/en/blog/best-ai-image-generation-editing-tools-2026
This is one blogger's subjective scoring with no published protocol. It is not usable as a benchmark.

### The strongest evidence: glyph-image conditioning measurably helps, including in editing models

This is the most decision-relevant finding of the whole pass.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** GlyphAnchor supplies explicit rendered character-shape conditioning (glyph patches) plus spatial cues, and measures the gain on both text-to-image and **image-editing** diffusion transformers.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02349

Reported gains, editing backbone (FireRed-Image-Edit-1.1):

- rare characters overall: +0.236;
- level-2 rare characters: 0.411 to 0.783, a +0.372 gain;
- level-3 rare characters: 0.067 to 0.416, a +0.349 gain;
- LongTextBench: 0.897 to 0.985 normalized edit distance, +0.088.

Reported gains, generation backbone (Z-Image):

- LongTextBench: 0.936 to 0.981, +0.045;
- CVTG-2K: 0.937 to 0.970, +0.033.

Two things follow directly.

First, supplying a rendered glyph image rather than relying on text-only prompting is empirically the right architecture, and the gain is largest exactly where the model is weakest.
Common glyphs improve by hundredths; rare and complex glyphs improve by three to four tenths.
Arabic name pendants with fused marks are the rare-glyph case.

Second, the benefit is demonstrated on editing models, not only generators, which matches our edits-endpoint pipeline.

Honest limits on this evidence, which matter:

- GlyphAnchor is a **trained conditioning module**, not a prompt technique. It fine-tunes the backbone to consume glyph patches. We cannot install it into `gpt-image-2`.
- The paper's benchmarks are English and Chinese. **It does not evaluate Arabic.** Extending the rare-glyph result to Arabic is my inference and is not measured in that paper.
- What transfers to us is the direction and the mechanism: pixel-level glyph shape carries information that text tokens do not. That is a strong argument for our deterministic canonical PNG being in the reference set on every call, and a weak argument about the exact magnitude we should expect.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Related literature confirms the mechanism is an active and effective research direction rather than an isolated result: Glyph-ByT5 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09622), Glyph-ByT5-v2 for multilingual rendering (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.10208), HDGlyph for long-tail text (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06543v1), and GlyphDraw2 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02252).

**[THIRD-PARTY]** On the recognition side, QARI-OCR reports a Word Error Rate of 0.160 and Character Error Rate of 0.061 on diacritically rich Arabic, with "superior handling of tashkeel (diacritical marks)."
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02295

Relevant to us as a **verifier** component, not a generator.
If we ever want an automated Arabic spelling gate that is not a VLM's opinion, an Arabic OCR with a published CER is the more auditable instrument.
I did not verify QARI-OCR's licence or deployability in the time budget.

### Dots and hamza specifically

I found **no** published study measuring i'jam (dot) or hamza preservation as a separate axis in generated images.
Every source that mentions diacritics does so qualitatively.
This is a genuine gap.
Our own comparison-based name check is currently better evidence than anything published, and we should treat dot count and hamza presence as our own defect categories rather than expecting the literature to supply a benchmark.

## 4. Evaluator-optimizer / generator-critic loops

### The loop shape

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The canonical structure, stated plainly by AWS: a generator produces an output, "an evaluator agent reviews the result using a critique prompt or evaluation rubric", then "the original agent or a new optimizer agent revises the output", and "the loop repeats until the result meets a set of criteria, is approved, or reaches a retry limit."
AWS explicitly recommends managing "feedback thresholds, retries, and fallback strategies" in the controller rather than inside the agent.
Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/agentic-ai-patterns/evaluator-reflect-refine-loop-patterns.html

Their worked example evaluates against named dimensions rather than a single score, for example "rate this output by clarity, completeness, and tone", with revision triggered when "the rating falls below a threshold."
Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/agentic-ai-patterns/evaluator-reflect-refine-loop-patterns.html

### How many attempts

**[THIRD-PARTY]** SELF-REFINE reports "improving by ~20% absolute on average in task performance" over one-step generation.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651
The abstract does not state an iteration count, so I cannot cite a number from it.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The most directly relevant paper is on iterative refinement for compositional **image** generation, with a critic that emits one of four actions - STOP, BACKTRACK, RESTART, CONTINUE - and a verifier that scores each candidate.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15286

Its compute-allocation result is the actionable one.
At a fixed budget of 16 generations, the best split is 8 iterative steps with 2 parallel samples, and pure serial iteration (16 steps, 1 sample) is worse at 69.2%.
Pure iteration also beats pure parallelism at small budgets: 48.4% for 4 iterations and 1 sample versus 41.1% for the parallel equivalent.
Humans preferred the iterative outputs 58.7% to 41.3% over the parallel baseline.
Reported gains versus the parallel baseline at matched compute were 16.9% on ConceptMix k=7 and 13.8% on T2I-CompBench 3D-Spatial.

The practical read for our lab: iteration beats brute-force resampling, but not indefinitely.
Around 8 refinement steps is where their optimum sat, and past that they were better off spending the budget on a second parallel seed.
The BACKTRACK and RESTART actions matter - a critic that can only say "try again" will chase a bad seed forever, and a critic that can say "this seed is unrecoverable, restart" converges faster.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The broader survey position is that "the largest improvements occur in the first 1-2 refinement rounds", with quality "saturated after approximately three feedback loops" in the studies summarised.
Source: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/iterative-self-refinement

These two sources disagree on where saturation sits (3 versus 8).
I am not going to paper over that.
The honest reading is that the saturation point is task-dependent, and the cheap way to find ours is to log the per-iteration QA verdict and observe where our own curve flattens, rather than picking a number from a paper about a different task.

### One axis per iteration

I found **no** paper that isolates "change one attribute per iteration" as a tested variable.
The image-refinement paper explicitly does the opposite: it "handles multiple attributes sequentially rather than one per iteration", with the critic proposing a targeted refinement prompt and then escalating it on the next round if the first attempt was insufficient - in their example, first "hide the mouse", then "the mouse should be almost entirely obscured."
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15286

So the supported pattern is not "one defect per iteration."
It is "one **targeted, escalating** instruction per iteration", where the critic re-states the same fix more forcefully if it did not land.
That is a meaningfully different loop design and it is cheap for us to adopt.

### Defect taxonomies used by others

**[THIRD-PARTY]** GIE-Bench's two-axis taxonomy is the most directly transferable: functional correctness (did the requested change happen) separated from content preservation (did anything else change that should not have).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11493

Scoring these separately is important for us.
A pendant image can be a perfect photograph and a total identity failure, and a single blended quality score hides that.

**[THIRD-PARTY]** The image-refinement paper's own error taxonomy has only two buckets, and both are about the loop rather than the image: "(i) Incorrect VLM reasoning" and "(ii) Inability of editor to make prompted changes."
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15286

This is worth logging in our own runs.
When our loop fails, we should record whether the critic was wrong or whether the critic was right and the generator could not comply.
Those two failures need opposite responses - fix the rubric, or stop retrying and escalate.

### The failure mode to design against

**[THIRD-PARTY]** Reward hacking in self-evaluating loops: "repeated self-evaluation leads the generator and in-context judge to jointly exploit weaknesses in their own scoring proxies, leading to rising judge ratings even as true human quality degrades."
Source: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/iterative-self-refinement

For us, the deterministic identity check must sit **outside** the model-judged loop.
If the same model family both generates and grades the Arabic spelling, a rising verifier score is not evidence of a correct pendant.
A pixel comparison against the canonical PNG, or an OCR with a published CER, is the non-gameable anchor.

### Adjacent note on the model itself

**[UNVERIFIED]** Several write-ups describe `gpt-image-2` as having a built-in "thinking mode" where the model can "plan layout, search the web for references, and self-check outputs" before generating.
Source: https://aimlapi.com/blog/gpt-image-2-release-date-features-and-everything-you-need-to-know
I could not confirm this against OpenAI's developer docs or announcement (the announcement URL returned 403).
If true it would partly overlap our outer critic loop, but it is not something to design against until verified.

## Directly actionable for tonight's lab

1. Label every reference by role and index in the prompt body, using fal's exact pattern - "Image 1: exact pendant silhouette to preserve. Image 2: lighting and finish anchor." - because unlabeled references make the model guess which is content and which is reference. https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

2. Put the canonical identity PNG **first** in the input array and A/B it against last, because the OpenAI cookbook says only the first input gets "extra richness in texture", and that privilege is documented for `gpt-image-1` but not re-confirmed for `gpt-image-2`. https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity

3. Replace negative-only instructions with an explicit positive preserve list plus a closing constraint, mirroring the pattern "Preserve the ... from Image 1" followed by "Keep everything else the same. No extra objects." https://fal.ai/learn/tools/prompting-gpt-image-2

4. 5. Test one composited reference sheet (silhouette plus anchor in a single image) against two separate references, since the only vendor-adjacent advice on multi-reference workflows is to composite rather than multiply inputs. https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity

6. Add the contact-shadow sentence verbatim to every packshot prompt - "a subtle contact shadow directly beneath the product, becoming softer and lighter further away" - as the cheapest single fix for the floating-object look. https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

7. Strip evaluative adjectives ("luxury", "premium", "elegant") from prompts and replace them with optical properties (grain direction, highlight behaviour, reflection variation), and state light direction together with its shadow and reflection consequence in one sentence, since realism comes from physical consistency rather than added detail. https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

8. Ban the CGI triggers explicitly in the shot profile - dead-centre symmetry, mirror-perfect reflections, and shadowless placement - and adapt the "subtle surface imperfections" cue to polishing lines and uneven speculars rather than scratches, which would read as damage on new gold. https://getimg.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-product-photos-look-real

9. Keep the rendered canonical Arabic PNG in the reference set on every single call and never fall back to text-only prompting for the name, because explicit glyph-shape conditioning produces its largest measured gains exactly on rare and complex glyphs in editing models (+0.372 on level-2 rare characters) - noting that paper measured English and Chinese, not Arabic. https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02349

10. Structure the retry loop as a critic emitting one targeted, escalating instruction per round with STOP / BACKTRACK / RESTART / CONTINUE available, cap iterations low and spend surplus budget on a second seed rather than a ninth retry, score functional correctness and identity preservation as two separate axes, and keep the deterministic identity gate outside the model-judged loop to avoid the documented reward-hacking failure. https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15286 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11493 and https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/iterative-self-refinement
